## Read this once before cooking

**The shortcuts are part of the recipe.** Use washed bagged greens, fresh pre-cut florets, plain shredded cabbage, jarred sauces, and already-thawed meat or fish. Frozen meat blocks and frozen vegetable substitutions do not inherit these timings. All weights are edible amounts after trimming, except frozen spinach, whose package weight is stated before thawing and squeezing. Heat spinach according to its package during prep, then cool until safe to handle and press dry.

**Start preheating immediately.** Estimates allow about 10–12 minutes for a conventional oven to preheat, overlapping prep. Use bake, not convection. Air fryer preheating usually overlaps prep too; follow your manual. A slower appliance or extra doneness cooking adds time. These are planning estimates, not a promise that every appliance finishes by minute 40.

**Serving changes do not preserve cooking capacity or time.** The comparison table describes the original four-adult batch. At six/eight servings, preserve single layers and dish depth using extra cookware or base-size batches; allow more time. Microwave potato loads need separate endpoint checks. Smaller two-serving egg layers need an earlier center check.

**Give food enough room.** Oven recipes use two 18×13-inch rimmed metal half-sheet pans unless a baking dish is specified. Put them on the upper-middle and lower-middle racks with space for air between them. Spread food in a single layer. Swap racks during the scheduled stir or addition. If a no-stir recipe browns unevenly in your oven, add one rack swap halfway. Smaller pans mean extra batches and longer dinner times.

Air fryer recipes are conditional on the full batch fitting as instructed. Check the empty baskets first. Four fish fillets or a pound of broccoli will not fit in every fryer. Use the oven fallback immediately if you would need to stack food. No loose parchment during preheating.

Prepare cold salad ingredients before handling raw poultry. Keep raw poultry, its bowl, board, and utensils away from ready-to-eat food. Wash hands afterward; use clean tongs, plates, and boards for cooked meat. Do not rinse raw poultry. These steps follow [USDA handling guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/chicken-farm-table).

Use a thermometer in the thickest part: chicken and ground turkey **165°F**, fish **145°F**, meat-free frittata **160°F**, and reheated cooked chicken **165°F**. Check several pieces or locations. Keep cooking if the timer ends first. See the [FoodSafety.gov temperature chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

Pressure instructions assume an **Instant Pot Duo 6QT covered by the Duo V5 manual**. It requires at least 1½ cups, or 375 mL, thin water-based liquid. Food stays below PC MAX; expanding foods use the lower fill limit. Your actual model's manual controls, especially for 8-quart or Ultimate Lid models. Low watching means doing nearby tasks while following the manual, not leaving the appliance unattended. [Duo manufacturer manual](https://p.widencdn.net/bbdh9b/113-0059-01_110-0043-01_112-0170-01_113-0011-01_112-0010-01_110-0003-01_IB_DuoV5_MANUAL_399-1235-03US_US_R42-FINAL-09-30-2025_Lo.pdf).

## Nutrition and keto portions

The estimates include every measured oil, sauce, seed topping, cheese, and salad ingredient. Family starches are separate. Meal recipes aim for roughly 25 g or more protein and at least 100 g non-starchy vegetables per person, measured as edible ingredients before cooking. Vegetable weights are not cooked serving weights, and canned tomato weights include juice.

For keto portions, the calculated target is 10 g net carbs or less. Net carbs here means US total carbohydrate minus fiber. Check your own daily carb budget and labels; this does not guarantee ketosis. Use plain strained Greek yogurt with at least 9 g protein and no more than 4 g total carbs per 100 g. Regular unstrained yogurt is not an equivalent protein substitute.

Calculations use [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/) and the specified manufacturer labels. They are rounded estimates, not lab measurements. Brands, meat fat, drained yields, and retained cooking liquid vary. Calories are shown because some of these are lighter dinners. Add food to suit your appetite; count any additions separately.

Smoothie nutrition is shown per estimated 8 fl oz glass. Finished yields are estimated from ingredient mass at approximately 1 g/ml, not measured blender output. Air, blender retention, and added liquid change the actual volume. Meal nutrition is shown for one quarter of the recipe.

**Ingredients**

- 680g Skin-on salmon fillets — raw, thawed; individual fillets about 1 inch thick. Choose individual fillets about 170 g (6 oz) each, keeping the stated thickness.
- 454g Asparagus — trimmed, medium stalks
- 27g Olive oil — as purchased. Half to asparagus; half to salmon.
- 3g Garlic powder. Divide equally.
- 3g Fine salt. Use the proportional split described in the method.
- to taste Black pepper
- 2g Lemon zest — finely grated. Use finely grated zest.
- 30g Lemon juice — unsweetened
- 20g Hemp hearts — hulled. Use hulled hemp hearts in the measured portion.

**Equipment and capacity**

- Two air fryers with room for four fillets in one basket and 454 g asparagus in the other
- Instant-read thermometer

**Method**

1. **Prepare.** Preheat both fryers to 390°F. Toss asparagus with half the oil, half the garlic, and half the salt. Spread in one basket without a dense pile.
2. **Cook.** Pat salmon dry. Rub with remaining oil, garlic, salt, pepper, and zest. Put skin-side down in the other basket with gaps between fillets. Start both together.
3. **Finish.** Check asparagus at 7 minutes; medium stalks usually need 7–10. Check salmon at 10 minutes; allow 10–14 to reach 145°F. Remove each when ready. Finish with lemon juice and hemp.

**Why it works**

Lemon brightens the salmon; hemp adds a mild nutty finish without another cooking step.

- Thin asparagus needs an earlier check.
- Keep the fillets skin-side down and apart.

**Capacity fallback**

If either basket cannot hold its full batch, use two half-sheet pans at 425°F: fish about 12–18 min, asparagus 10–15 min; allow 25–35 min total.

**First-cook checks and limits**

- Basket fit and actual thickness can change the time; first cook has not been kitchen-tested.
- Check every packaged ingredient label for additional allergens; this data is not an allergy guarantee.

## Use the boosters where they help

Hemp belongs in a measured finish or mixed into eggs. Most dinners here use just ½ tbsp per person; the frittata uses 1 tbsp. Ground flax has a job as the turkey binder. Nutritional yeast finishes broccoli, potatoes, and gnocchi after cooking; it does not go under pressure or spend the whole roast burning on a pan.

The yeast estimate uses [Bob's Red Mill's label](https://www.bobsredmill.com/product/nutritional-yeast): 2 tbsp is 8 g. Your brand's flakes may weigh differently. Check fortification if you want B12 from it. Flax supplies plant omega-3 fat, ALA; that differs from fish's marine omega-3 fats. [Harvard's omega-3 guide](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/what-should-you-eat/fats-and-cholesterol/types-of-fat/omega-3-fats/) explains the distinction.

## A small rotation that uses the equipment well

Start with salmon, fajitas, chickpea bowls, and the chicken-lentil bake. That covers quick fish, a shared family main, a vegetarian dinner, and a nearly literal dump-and-go dinner. Keep the frittata and gnocchi for evenings with a little more oven time.

Two air fryers help when protein and vegetables need different stop times. The oven is often easier for four portions than multiple fryer batches. The second Instant Pot is useful for a separate family starch when you have time to follow a tested rice recipe; dry-rice cooking is outside the listed timelines. Ready rice is faster on a deadline. There is no need to run every appliance just because it is available.

Reuse chicken thighs, broccoli, cauliflower, bagged greens, Greek yogurt, lemons, and limes. Pesto, salsa verde, harissa, and a few dry spices change the flavor without adding sauce work. Keep rice pouches, pita, and tortillas separate from the keto bases.

## Leftovers

Refrigerate cooked food in shallow containers within two hours, or one hour above 90°F. Use refrigerated leftovers within 3–4 days; freeze what you will not eat in that window. Reheat to 165°F. These limits follow [USDA storage guidance](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/How-do-I-handle-leftovers-safely) and the [FoodSafety.gov temperature chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

Keep greens and yogurt sauces separate from cooked leftovers. Portion the cooked main before adding rice or bread so tomorrow's keto serving remains easy to identify.
